# The story

**Spoilers.** This is the whole story of the game, the ending included.

It was first written on 12 November 2026, before any gameplay was built, because Eshan stopped the build and asked for it: "We need everything to happen for a meaning, flashbacks, backstory, reason behind every action we make the player do ... and the sudden feeling of everything clicking at the very end of the game. Build everything around it."

**Status: approved by Eshan on 13 November 2026** ("Yes, build it"), **and revised on 14 November after he played it from start to finish.** His verdict: "It was good ngl but the thing is it wasn't horror at all like from whcih angle did it scare or where was the suspense. WE NEED IT TO BE SCARIER!" He asked for "intense scenes with risers, small jumpscares, and music", "a chase scene or a hiding scene ... from an anomaly pretending to be Saad", tapes "showing us working with some standing and watching, the unknown has entered our house", a client who talks "very straightforward not like a human being", chats that are "slower and simpler to understand", and, in place of the old messages at the end, "cinematics with subtitles for story telling ... where we make everything click", with driving music, "fast paced when its all ending". He ended: "Think and work."

So this revision keeps what happened and how it ends, and changes how it is told: the middle is now frightening, and the end is a cutscene instead of a thread of old messages. What changed is listed under "What the 14 November revision changed". The version he approved on the 13th is in the history of this file.

Still his to decide, and listed at the bottom: the names, the title, how the two of them text, the setting, and now whether the thing with the light should turn out to be what this file says it is.

His five beats are all still here, in his order: the flat, the chores, the phone, the edit, the payoff.

## The story in one minute

**What the player is told.** You are a freelance video editor, alone in a flat that is too big for one person. It is late. You do the last chores of the day. A number you do not know offers far too much money for a small job, in flat sentences that do not sound like a person: three clips, something goes wrong in each one, find the frame and mark it. The clips are of empty rooms. Every time you scrub back they have changed, and they look more and more like your own flat. Then there is somebody in them: a pale figure in a grey hoodie, standing dead still, watching. It looks like you. The last clip is live. It shows you at your desk, and somebody else behind you, nearer every time you look. Then the lights go out, and something with a light where its face should be walks through the flat, looking for you.

**What is really going on.** You died in this flat a year ago tonight, at 3:07 in the morning. You have repeated your last night ever since without knowing it. The chores at the start of the game are the haunting: a rubbish bag that crosses an empty corridor by itself, a bolt that slides shut in an empty flat, a gas ring that lights itself. The footage is real. It was filmed in your flat after it was cleared out, by the one person who cannot let you go. He is the unknown number. The pale figure on the tapes is you: it is what a camera sees of you when you stand still. The thing with the light is him, tonight, walking through the dark flat with a head torch, looking for his friend.

**The part that should hurt.** The player did it themselves. In the first three minutes they bolt the front door and walk away from a pan of chai on a lit ring, with their own hands, because the game asked them to. Those are the two things that killed him. And then they spend the rest of the night hiding from the only person who came.

**The idea underneath.** A haunting is a recording that keeps playing. He is an editor. Tonight somebody finally sends him the footage, and he has to find the cut.

## Who

- **Saad** (working name). The player. A freelance video editor. Never heard. Seen only on tape: a pale figure in the grey hoodie and headphones he died in, never a face.
- **Hamza** (working name). His oldest friend. They shared this flat and ran a two-man video business out of the study: Hamza shot, Saad cut. The spare room was Hamza's. In the flat the player walks through, he is seen only by the light he carries.

Setting: a city in Pakistan, never named. November, cold, every window shut.

## What really happened, in order

1. **Two friends.** They rent the flat after university and work from it for four years. The film festival poster and the certificate in the study are from the short film they made together. So is the poster over the sofa: a figure standing in a lit doorway.
2. **The fight.** Hamza takes a salaried job abroad. Saad takes it as being left behind. They fight in the corridor. Hamza goes, and never comes back for his last boxes. Saad deletes his number, stays on in a flat too big for one, and takes every job he can get to cover both halves of the rent.
3. **The night: 16 November 2026.** The date is ringed on the kitchen calendar. It is a deadline.
   - 00:40. He takes out the rubbish. 00:41. He bolts the front door.
   - 01:00. He puts a pan of chai on the hob to stay awake.
   - 01:04. His phone buzzes in the study. A number he does not know: "Are you still up?" It is Hamza, with a new number. He does not answer. He sits down at the desk, puts his headphones on, and falls asleep.
   - 01:10. The chai boils over and puts out the flame. The gas keeps coming.
   - 03:07. Hamza is at the door. His old key turns the lock, but the bolt is on. He hammers on the door. He looks in through the peephole. He rings the landline and can hear it ringing inside. He smells gas. Saad dies at 3:07, asleep, with his friend hammering on his door.
4. **The year after.** The flat is cleared out. Hamza comes home for good and cannot let the flat go, so he pays the rent on it, empty. The woman downstairs hears someone walking about up there. He finds the bolt on, from the inside. He finds a ring lit. He is a cameraman, so he does the one thing he knows how to do: he puts a camera on a tripod and films the empty rooms at night. On the tapes, sometimes, there is a pale figure standing in the dark.
5. **Tonight: 16 November 2027.** One year on. Hamza sits in the empty flat, in the dark, with his footage, and sends the message he has sent every night: "Are you still up?" For the first time, an answer comes back: "Who is this?" He does not dare say. The last time he said who he was, he got silence. So he hides behind the one thing Saad never turned down: a client, a job, and money. He writes like a form, because he does not trust himself to write like himself.

What Hamza needs from the job is one answer. For a year he has believed that if he had come sooner, or broken the door down faster, Saad would be alive. The frames Saad marks tell him the truth: the bolt went on at 00:41 and the flame went out at 01:10, two hours before he knocked. It was the chai. It was not him.

## The rules

These hold everywhere in the game. Anything new has to obey them.

1. **The flat you walk through is the night Saad remembers.** It is always 3:07 there.
2. **The tape shows what is really there.** The camera is always right.
3. **Scrubbing forward plays the tape. Scrubbing back takes it further back than it should go:** past its own first frame, through the year, to the night.
4. **Saad cannot leave the flat, has no reflection, and is never heard. A camera catches him only when he stands still:** a pale figure in a grey hoodie and headphones, never a face. When he moves a thing, the tape shows only the thing. (The player takes the figure for a stranger who looks like him.)
5. **Hamza is alive, and there is no monster.** Nobody is trying to hurt anybody. Everything frightening is Hamza, or Saad, or that night coming back. But until the cutscene the player must believe the opposite: that something is in the flat, that it looks like him, and that it is hunting him.
6. **In the flat, the living are seen only by their light.** When the night Saad remembers gives way and its lights go out, what he sees of Hamza is a dark shape with a light where the face should be: a man wearing a head torch.
7. **Nothing before the cutscene says in words that Saad is dead.** The cutscene says it plainly.
8. **Everything the unknown number says must be frightening the first time and true the second.** "I can see you." "I know you are in here." "It is Hamza."

## The game, beat by beat

About 18 minutes. The words in quotes are what the task line says.

### Part 1. An ordinary night (about 3 minutes)

The flat feels safe. That is the job of this part, and the trap. No music.

1. **Start.** The study, behind the desk chair. Every clock in the flat says 3:07. "Take out the rubbish."
2. **The rubbish.** Pick up the full bag by the kitchen bin, carry it down the corridor, put it out. The front door opens a crack onto a dark landing. The door shuts.
3. **The bolt.** "Lock up." Slide the bolt on the front door.
4. **The chai.** "Make chai." Light the ring under the pan. "Wait for it to boil."
5. **The phone.** A few seconds later his phone buzzes in the study, loud enough to hear from the kitchen. "Your phone is buzzing in the study." The chai line is the only one in the game that never gets ticked off. The player walks away from a lit ring. The game does not mention it again until the end.

### Part 2. The job (about 9 minutes)

A low drone comes in under the flat with the first message, and rises a little with every tape.

6. **The unknown number.** Pick up the phone. A short exchange (the script is below): flat, exact, no feeling. Three files, one thing wrong in each, a great deal of money, half of it sent now. "Sit down at the PC."
7. **How the edit works.** Each clip is a locked-off shot of an empty room at night, with a date and time burnt into the corner. Something moves in it, but the moment it moves is lost in a burst of tape static. So the player has to scrub back to find it, and every time they scrub back the room has more of Saad's things in it, and the date in the corner is earlier. When the moment is on screen, they mark it. Nothing on the timeline says where it is, it is in a different place on every tape, and a worn tape drops out to static in other places too.
8. **Clip 1: the corridor.** Dated August 2027: next year. A bare corridor that could be anywhere. Scrub back: a doormat, a shoe rack, pictures on the wall. It is his corridor. On one pass a pale figure in a grey hoodie stands at the far end by the front door, dead still, facing the camera. On the next it is gone. A black rubbish bag slides down the corridor with nobody carrying it. Mark it. "Correct. Next file."
9. **The landline.** In the corridor behind him the landline rings, three times, and stops. Nobody has that number.
10. **Clip 2: the front door.** Scrub back: on one pass the figure is right there, a metre from the lens, its back to the camera, facing the door. On the next it is gone, and the bolt slides across by itself. Mark it. Then the tape jumps on to 3:07 and something hammers on the door from outside, hard enough to shake it.
11. **The door, for real.** The same hammering, from his own front door, down the corridor behind him. The player gets up and walks the corridor. It stops when he is close. Through the peephole: a dim landing, the neighbours' door, the stairs. Nobody. Then a face fills the lens, looking in. The jump scare. "Correct. I heard you go to the door."
12. **Clip 3: the kitchen.** A ring on the hob lights by itself. Scrub back: on one pass the figure stands at the hob with its back to the camera, looking down at the ring, waiting. Scrub back again: a pan is on it. The chai rises, boils over, and the flame goes out. Mark the frame where the flame dies. In the real kitchen, the pan boils over: a hiss the player can hear from the study. From now on the gas hisses. "1:10. Correct." Then: "One more file. This one is live."
13. **Clip 4: the study, live.** An empty room, and in it somebody sitting at a desk that is not there, in a grey hoodie and headphones, hands out in front of him. Far off, through two doorways, someone else is sitting in a chair, watching. Scrub back: a desk, a monitor, a chair. It is this room. The time in the corner is tonight's, and it is running. The figure at the desk is the player. Each time he scrubs back, the other one is nearer: the spare room doorway, the corridor, the study doorway, right beside the desk, bending to look at the screen. If the player turns round in the real room, nobody is there. A riser climbs with every step.
14. **All the way back.** The date falls back to 16 November 2026, 3:07. The figure at the desk is slumped onto it, asleep, in the same hoodie, headphones on. Then the tape tears back to tonight, live: the one beside the desk straightens and turns to the camera, and a light comes on where his face is. The picture burns out to white. "I can see you."

### Part 3. The light (about 3 minutes)

15. **Lights out.** Every light in the flat dies at once, and the monitor with it. The night he remembers is giving way. Beside the desk, an arm's length away, stands a dark figure with a white light where its face should be, bent over the dead screen. "Hide."
16. **Out of the study.** The player stands up and gets out behind its back. If he stays, it turns, and the light finds him.
17. **The search.** It comes out into the corridor and goes from room to room, the light sweeping each one: the spare room, the bedroom, the bathroom, the store room, the kitchen. Its footsteps can be heard through the walls. The player hides: in a wardrobe, looking out through the gap between its doors, or by keeping a wall between himself and the light. If the light falls on him from across the flat, it comes for him, fast, and he has to get out of its sight. Messages buzz in his pocket: "Where are you." "I know you are in here." "It is Hamza."
18. **Found.** If the light falls on him up close, the picture burns white, the tape rewinds, and it is lights out again. (A haunting is a recording that keeps playing.)
19. **It stops.** At the front door it stands still. "Please." The light goes out.

### Part 4. The cut (about 3 minutes)

20. **The cutscene.** The tape, full screen, cut fast to music, with Hamza's words as subtitles (the script is below). The man with the light switches it off: it is only a man. One year ago tonight: the bolt, the chai, the flame going out, the headphones, the hammering, 3:07. Every night since: the rubbish, the bolt, the chai. The figure on the tapes is you. I was not hunting you. I was looking for you. This is where it clicks: every chore, every tape and every frightening message was the same night, seen from the other side of the door.
21. **The gas.** "Turn off the gas." The lights are back and the bulb burns steady. The music drives. In the kitchen the hiss is loud. Turn the knob. Silence. The clock on the microwave changes to 3:08.
22. **The door.** "Open the door." Slide the bolt back. The door opens. The landing light is on, and for the first time it is a place: a stairwell, the neighbours' door, his rubbish bag where he left it.
23. **The last shot.** On tape. The empty flat, 16 November 2027. The front door stands open with light coming through it, and Hamza stands in the corridor looking at it: a figure in a lit doorway, the same picture as the poster of their film. The time in the corner turns from 3:07 to 3:08. STOP. The title. Credits.

## The scares, and what each one is

The player should spend Parts 2 and 3 believing that something has been inside their home, that it looks like them, and that it is in there now. Every scare is true to that fear, and true to the ending.

1. Footage that changes when you scrub back.
2. The footage is of your own flat. Someone has been in here with a camera.
3. The landline, ringing in the dark corridor. (Hamza, a year ago, outside the door.)
4. A pale figure on the tape: at the end of the corridor, at the front door, at the hob. There on one pass, gone on the next. (Saad.)
5. Hammering on the front door at 3 a.m. Then a face at the peephole. (Hamza, a year ago.)
6. The pan boiling over in the real kitchen, heard from the study.
7. The live clip: somebody at your desk, and somebody behind him, nearer every time you look. (Saad, and Hamza, tonight.)
8. The body at the desk. The light. The lights going out.
9. The thing with the light for a face, an arm's length away, and then hunting through the flat. (Hamza, with a head torch, looking for his friend.)
10. The messages while you hide.
11. The hiss.

He has been afraid, all night, of himself, and of the only person who stayed.

## The music

Every sound is made by code; there are no sound files.

- **The title and the credits:** a slow, sad theme.
- **Part 1:** nothing. Room tone.
- **Part 2:** a low drone that rises with each tape; a quiet cold note the first time the figure is on screen; a riser on the live tape; a hard hit on the face at the peephole and when the lights go out.
- **Part 3:** the drone at full, a heartbeat that quickens as the light comes near, a hit when it finds him.
- **Part 4:** a fast, driving piece under the cutscene and the last two jobs: a restless figure over a pulsing bass, building layer by layer. Eshan asked for "a music like L's theme A from death note or something similar". It is an original piece in that spirit, not that tune. It cuts dead when the gas goes off. The sad theme comes back when the door opens.

## What the 14 November revision changed

Kept: who they are, what happened that night, the chores, the four tapes, the door, the gas, the bolt, the last shot. The ending is the same ending.

- **The client** no longer sounds like a man with a secret. He sounds like a form. The human lines ("You were always the one who could see it") are gone from the phone and come back, plainly, in the cutscene.
- **Saad is seen on the tapes** (rule 4). Before, he was never seen until the last frame, and the chair on the live tape was empty.
- **Hamza is seen in the flat**, by his light (rule 6), and the player hides from him. Before, nobody was ever there when the player turned round.
- **The peephole** shows a place, and then a face. Before: black, and nobody.
- **The landline rings.** It was on the cut list.
- **The thread of old messages is gone.** A cutscene says the same things in twenty short lines, with pictures. The year of 3:07 messages is no longer in the game.
- **There is music.**
- **A slip, mended:** the first version said the bolt went on both at 00:40 and at 00:52. It is 00:41, as on the tape.

## What only Eshan can decide

1. **Is the thing with the light allowed to be Hamza?** He asked for "an anomaly pretending to be Saad". The player gets one, for ten minutes: a figure that looks like him on the tapes, and a thing with a light for a face that hunts him. Then the cutscene takes both away: the figure was him, and the hunter was his friend. That keeps the ending he approved. If he wants a real monster that stays a monster, the ending has to change, and he should say so.
2. **The names.** Saad and Hamza are placeholders.
3. **The title.** Decided, 16 November: the game is called "Untitled", the working title it had all along. He turned down the three the story offered: "Still Up?" is the first message of the night, the message Hamza sends every night for a year, and what a ghost is. "3:07" is the clue that is on every clock. "Unknown Number" is the plainest.
4. **How they text.** Plain English now, with the Urdu gone from the phone. Say if some should come back.
5. **The setting.** A city in Pakistan that is never named, and so the money is in rupees. Say if it should be somewhere else.
